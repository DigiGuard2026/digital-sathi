📰 Fake News Awareness

LEARN → PRACTICE → QUIZ → SCORE → PROGRESS

Simple Explanation

Fake news is false or misleading information shared as if it were true. It can spread quickly, especially through social media and messaging apps.

Practical Examples

  • A forwarded message with an alarming claim may have no real source behind it.
  • Checking multiple trusted news sources helps confirm whether something is actually true.
  • Doctored images or old photos are sometimes shared out of context to mislead people.

Safety Tips

  • Check a trusted news source before believing or sharing anything surprising.
  • Many forwards do not mean something is true.
  • Use fact-checking websites to verify claims.
  • Be cautious of ALL CAPS or urgent-sounding headlines.
  • Think twice before sharing — verify first.

What Is Misinformation?
  • Misinformation is information that is false or inaccurate, whether or not someone meant to mislead you.
  • Disinformation is false information created and shared on purpose to deceive people.
  • Misinformation can spread through social media, messaging apps, TV, or word of mouth.
  • Even well-meaning people can accidentally spread misinformation by sharing without checking.
  • Misinformation can affect health decisions, elections, money matters, and personal safety.
  • Always pause and think before believing or sharing anything that sounds surprising.
Fake News vs Misleading Information
  • Fake news is completely made-up or false content presented as real news.
  • Misleading information may contain some truth but is presented in a way that creates a false impression.
  • A real photo or video used with a wrong caption or date is misleading, not fully fake.
  • Fake news often uses fake websites, fake logos, or fake journalist names to look real.
  • Misleading headlines can exaggerate or twist a true story to grab attention.
  • Both types can cause confusion, so it's important to verify before trusting either.
How to Verify a Claim
  • Search the claim in a search engine to see if trusted news outlets are reporting it too.
  • Look for the same news on at least two or three reliable, well-known sources.
  • Use fact-checking websites (like PIB Fact Check, Alt News, or Boom Live) to check viral claims.
  • Be extra careful with claims that sound too shocking, too good, or too urgent to be true.
  • Check if the claim includes real names, dates, and places that can be verified.
  • If you can't confirm a claim from a trusted source, don't share it yet.
Checking the Source
  • Look at who published the information — is it a known, trusted news organization?
  • Check the website address (URL) carefully; fake sites often use names similar to real ones.
  • Look for an "About Us" page and contact details to see if the source is genuine.
  • Be cautious of posts from anonymous accounts or pages with no clear identity.
  • Official government information usually comes from .gov.in websites or verified social media accounts.
  • A source with many spelling mistakes or poor design is often less trustworthy.
Reverse-Image-Search Concept
  • Reverse image search lets you check where a photo first appeared online.
  • It helps you find out if a photo is old, from a different event, or used out of context.
  • On a phone or computer, you can search an image using Google Lens or Google Images.
  • Save or screenshot the image, then upload it to the search tool to see matching results.
  • If the same image appears with different captions or dates, it may be used to mislead.
  • Reverse image search is especially useful for viral photos linked to accidents, riots, or disasters.
Checking Dates & Context
  • Always check the date of a photo, video, or news article before believing or sharing it.
  • Old news or old images are sometimes reshared as if they are happening right now.
  • Read the full article, not just the headline, to understand the real context.
  • Check if the event mentioned actually happened in the place and time claimed.
  • Look for updates or corrections — sometimes an early report changes as more facts come out.
  • A video clip taken out of its original context can create a completely false impression.
AI-Generated Content & Deepfakes
  • Deepfakes are AI-generated videos or audio that make people appear to say or do things they never did.
  • AI can also create fake but realistic-looking photos of people, places, or events that never existed.
  • Look for unnatural blinking, odd lighting, blurry edges, or mismatched lip movements in videos.
  • Be cautious of voice messages or calls that sound urgent and ask for money — voices can be cloned.
  • Reverse image search and fact-checking tools can sometimes help identify AI-generated images.
  • If a video or audio clip seems shocking or unusual for that person, verify it before believing it.
Fake Screenshots
  • Screenshots of news, chats, or social media posts can be easily edited using simple apps.
  • A fake screenshot can show a message, tweet, or headline that was never actually posted.
  • Check if the same message or post can be found directly on the original app or website.
  • Look for mismatched fonts, spacing, or logos, which can be signs of editing.
  • Be cautious of screenshots without dates, usernames, or verified account marks.
  • If a screenshot cannot be verified elsewhere, treat it as unconfirmed.
Identifying Manipulated Content
  • Manipulated content includes edited photos, cropped images, or videos with altered speed or sound.
  • Cropping a photo or video can remove important context and change its meaning.
  • Look closely for blurred areas, odd shadows, or straight lines that look bent — signs of editing.
  • Compare the content with the original source, if you can find it, to spot differences.
  • Be cautious of content that seems designed to provoke anger, fear, or strong reactions.
  • When in doubt, ask: "Who benefits if I believe and share this?"
Before-You-Share Checklist
  • Have I checked this news on at least one trusted, well-known source?
  • Do I know who created or posted this content, and is it a genuine source?
  • Have I checked the date to make sure this is current, not old news?
  • Have I looked at the full context, not just the headline or a short clip?
  • If the content is a photo or video, have I checked if it looks edited or AI-generated?
  • If I'm still unsure, it's safer not to share until I can verify it.
