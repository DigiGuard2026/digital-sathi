Simple Explanation

UPI (Unified Payments Interface) lets you send and receive money instantly using just your phone — no need to carry cash.

Practical Examples

  • To pay a shopkeeper, you scan their QR code and enter your UPI PIN to send money.
  • Your UPI PIN is like a signature — it authorizes a payment, so it must stay private.
  • If a payment fails but money is deducted, it's usually auto-reversed within a few days.

Safety Tips

  • Never share your UPI PIN or OTP with anyone.
  • You only scan a QR code to SEND money, never to receive it.
  • Always check the receiver's name before confirming payment.
  • Check your bank SMS alerts regularly for unknown transactions.
  • Use only well-known, official payment apps.

UPI Basics
  • UPI lets you send and receive money instantly using just your phone.
  • You need a bank account linked to your mobile number to use UPI.
  • Popular UPI apps include Google Pay, PhonePe, Paytm, and BHIM.
  • Your UPI ID looks like a name followed by @ and a bank or app name.
  • You don't need to share your bank account number for most UPI payments.
  • UPI works 24x7, including weekends and holidays, with no extra charge.
QR Payments
  • A QR code is a small square you scan with your phone's camera to pay.
  • Open your UPI app, tap "Scan QR", and point your camera at the code.
  • Check the receiver's name and amount carefully before confirming payment.
  • Enter your UPI PIN to complete the payment after scanning.
  • Scanning a QR code is only for sending money, never for receiving it.
  • Never scan a QR code to "receive" money, a refund, or cashback — that's a scam.
Sending & Receiving Money
  • To send money, enter the receiver's UPI ID or phone number in your app.
  • Always check the receiver's name shown on screen before sending.
  • Enter the amount and your UPI PIN to complete the transfer.
  • To receive money, just share your UPI ID or QR code — no PIN needed.
  • You'll get an SMS or app notification once money is received.
  • Money usually reaches the other person's account within seconds.
Checking Transaction Status
  • Open your UPI app and go to "Transaction History" to see past payments.
  • Each entry shows the date, amount, and status: success, pending, or failed.
  • "Pending" means the transaction is still processing — wait a few minutes.
  • You can also confirm payments through your bank's SMS alerts.
  • Save the transaction ID if you ever need to raise a complaint.
  • Check your bank statement or passbook if you're unsure about a payment.
UPI PIN
  • Your UPI PIN is a secret 4- or 6-digit code that authorizes every payment.
  • You set your UPI PIN when you first link your bank account in the app.
  • Never share your UPI PIN with anyone, including bank staff or family.
  • You only enter your UPI PIN to SEND money, never to receive it.
  • Use "Forgot UPI PIN" in your app to reset it if you forget.
  • Change your PIN immediately if you think someone else may know it.
OTP Safety
  • An OTP (One-Time Password) is a code sent by SMS to verify it's really you.
  • OTPs are meant only for you — never share them, even with bank callers.
  • Banks and payment apps never call or message asking for your OTP.
  • Anyone asking for your OTP to "help" you is almost always a scammer.
  • An OTP usually expires within a few minutes for your safety.
  • Never enter an OTP on a link or website you weren't expecting.
Payment Requests
  • A payment request asks you to approve sending money to someone.
  • You never need your UPI PIN just to RECEIVE money.
  • Scammers sometimes disguise a "collect request" as a way to receive money.
  • Read the request screen carefully — it will clearly say "Pay" if money will leave your account.
  • Never enter your UPI PIN for a request you didn't expect.
  • When in doubt, call the person directly before approving any request.
Failed/Wrong Transactions
  • If a payment fails but money is deducted, it's usually auto-reversed in a few days.
  • Note the transaction ID and time as soon as a payment fails.
  • Check your transaction history and bank SMS to confirm what happened.
  • Contact your bank or app support if money isn't refunded in 3-5 working days.
  • If you sent money to the wrong person, contact your bank right away.
  • Keep screenshots and reference numbers ready when contacting support.
Common Payment Scams
  • Fake "refund" or "cashback" offers that ask you to scan a QR code or enter your PIN.
  • Calls pretending to be your bank, asking for your OTP, PIN, or card details.
  • Messages warning your account will be blocked unless you click a link now.
  • Fake customer care numbers found through search engines instead of official sources.
  • Requests to install "screen sharing" apps to help fix a payment problem.
  • Anyone who creates urgency and pressures you to act immediately is likely a scammer.
What to Do If Something Goes Wrong
  • Stay calm and stop sharing any more information with the caller or sender.
  • Change your UPI PIN immediately if you think it may be compromised.
  • Call your bank's official helpline (from their website or card) to report it.
  • Use the "Report/Block" option in your UPI app for suspicious transactions.
  • Report cyber fraud on the National Cybercrime Helpline: 1930 or cybercrime.gov.in.
  • File a written complaint with your bank and keep a copy for your records.
