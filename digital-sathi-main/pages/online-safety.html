🔐 Online Safety

LEARN → PRACTICE → QUIZ → SCORE → PROGRESS

Simple Explanation

Staying safe online means protecting your passwords, personal information, and being alert to common tricks scammers use.

Practical Examples

  • A strong, unique password for each account makes it much harder for hackers to break in.
  • Two-factor authentication adds an extra step, like a code sent to your phone, for extra security.
  • Phishing messages look official but are designed to steal your information — always double-check before clicking.

Safety Tips

  • Use a unique, strong password for each account.
  • Turn on two-step verification wherever available.
  • Never share your OTP or password with anyone.
  • Don't click links from unknown senders.
  • Review your social media privacy settings regularly.

Strong Passwords
  • Use a different, unique password for every important account.
  • A strong password mixes uppercase, lowercase, numbers, and symbols.
  • Avoid using your name, birth date, or common words as your password.
  • Make passwords at least 8-10 characters long — longer is safer.
  • Never write your password on paper or share it in a message.
  • Consider using a password manager to remember strong passwords safely.
Two-Factor Authentication (2FA)
  • 2FA adds a second security step after your password, like an OTP or app code.
  • Turn on 2FA for email, banking, and social media accounts wherever available.
  • Even if someone learns your password, 2FA can still keep your account safe.
  • Common 2FA methods include SMS codes, authenticator apps, or fingerprint checks.
  • Never share your 2FA code with anyone, even someone claiming to be support staff.
  • Set up backup options for 2FA in case you lose your phone.
Phishing
  • Phishing is a trick where fake messages or emails try to steal your personal information.
  • Phishing messages often create urgency, like "Act now or your account will be closed."
  • Check the sender's email address or number carefully — it often looks slightly wrong.
  • Legitimate companies never ask for your password or OTP through email or SMS.
  • Look for spelling mistakes or odd formatting — common signs of phishing.
  • When in doubt, contact the company directly using their official website or number.
Suspicious Links
  • Hover over or long-press a link to preview the actual web address before clicking.
  • Be cautious of shortened links (like bit.ly) that hide the real destination.
  • Avoid clicking links in unexpected messages, even from known contacts.
  • Fake websites often use URLs that look almost like the real one, with small changes.
  • Never enter your password or personal details after clicking an unknown link.
  • If a link seems too good to be true (free prizes, huge discounts), it's likely a scam.
Privacy Settings
  • Privacy settings control who can see your personal information and posts online.
  • Set your social media profiles to "Friends only" instead of fully public.
  • Regularly review which apps and websites have access to your accounts.
  • Turn off location sharing for apps that don't need to know where you are.
  • Limit the personal details you share publicly, like your phone number or address.
  • Check privacy settings again after app updates, as they sometimes reset.
App Permissions
  • Permissions control what an app can access, like your camera, contacts, or location.
  • Only grant permissions an app truly needs to work properly.
  • Review app permissions regularly in your phone's Settings menu.
  • Be cautious of apps asking for permissions unrelated to their purpose.
  • Deny a permission if unsure — most apps will ask again only if needed.
  • Uninstall apps you no longer use to reduce unnecessary access to your data.
Public Wi-Fi
  • Public Wi-Fi (in cafes, airports, malls) is often unsecured and riskier than home internet.
  • Avoid online banking or entering passwords while connected to public Wi-Fi.
  • Turn off auto-connect to Wi-Fi so your phone doesn't join unknown networks automatically.
  • Use mobile data instead of public Wi-Fi for anything sensitive, if possible.
  • A VPN can add extra protection when you must use public Wi-Fi.
  • Forget public Wi-Fi networks after use so your phone doesn't reconnect automatically.
Social-Media Safety
  • Think twice before sharing personal details like your address, travel plans, or daily routine.
  • Be cautious accepting friend requests from people you don't know in real life.
  • Avoid sharing photos of documents like ID cards, tickets, or bank details.
  • Report and block accounts that harass, threaten, or seem suspicious.
  • Check tagged photos and posts regularly to control what's visible on your profile.
  • Think before you post — once shared online, content can be hard to fully remove.
Account Recovery
  • Set up a recovery email and phone number for your important accounts in advance.
  • Recovery options let you regain access if you forget your password or lose your phone.
  • Keep your recovery information updated whenever you change your number or email.
  • Note down or save any backup codes given when you set up 2FA.
  • If you lose access, use the platform's official "Forgot Password" or "Account Recovery" option.
  • Be alert — scammers sometimes pretend to help with "account recovery" to steal access.
Cybercrime Reporting
  • If you face online fraud or harassment, report it as soon as possible.
  • You can report cybercrime online at cybercrime.gov.in or call the helpline 1930.
  • Save evidence like screenshots, messages, and transaction details before reporting.
  • Report suspicious or fraudulent accounts directly on the social media platform too.
  • Inform your bank immediately if the cybercrime involves money or account details.
  • Local police stations also accept cybercrime complaints if online reporting isn't possible.
